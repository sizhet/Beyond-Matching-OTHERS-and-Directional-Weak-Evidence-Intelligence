## Release Overview

Conventional matching and distance-scoring approaches are powerful when relevant features, metrics, and comparison spaces have already been defined. Yet intelligent judgments also depend on contextual, relational, reputational, prospective, and other weak or incompletely formalized evidence. This project proposes **OTHERS** as an open evidence space and develops a research framework for bringing such signals into accountable, directional scoring without confusing tentative evidence with established fact.

The central research question is:

> **How can intelligence evaluate what it cannot yet formally match?**

The proposed answer combines **Directional Measurement Sails**, **Reputational Intelligence**, **Perspective Distance**, **Open Structural Scoring Growth**, and **Counter-Evidence / Governance**. The architecture separates evidence assessment from perspective-dependent interpretation and authorized action selection.

## Key Contributions of v1.0.0

1. **Beyond Matching:** distinguishes feature matching, mathematical distance, perspective-relative assessment, and action-oriented scoring; explains why `distance = 1 - similarity` is not a universal metric or decision rule.
2. **OTHERS Open Evidence Space:** defines an extensible interface for heterogeneous signals that current matching schemes do not yet capture adequately.
3. **Directional Measurement Sails:** proposes direction-specific responses to weak evidence, with explicit attention to reliability, uncertainty, and correlation among signals.
4. **Reputational and Collective Weak Signals:** studies how distributed experience and indirect social signals can inform judgments while remaining vulnerable to cascades, duplication, and manipulation.
5. **Perspective and Competitive Action Scoring:** distinguishes evidence from goals, utility, risk, and the authority to choose; connects to Subjective Structural Intelligence (SSI).
6. **Open Structural Scoring Growth:** proposes a pathway from residual weak signals to tested candidate features, metrics, CCCs, and principles, with unsuccessful candidates remaining contestable.
7. **Evidence Governance:** introduces provenance, counter-evidence, promotion gates, audit records, and policy boundaries for scoring and structural updates.
8. **Unified Research and Validation Agenda:** sets out falsifiable hypotheses, baseline comparisons, ablations, and a staged research roadmap.

## Scientific Scope and Limitations

This release is a **research proposal**, not a benchmark result or proof of superiority over existing matching, ranking, multi-criteria decision analysis, or reputation systems. Its components overlap with established work in weak-signal analysis, social information processing, outranking methods, decision theory, and machine learning. The research contribution being explored is their integration into an **open, direction-sensitive, provenance-aware, structurally growing scoring architecture**.

Weak evidence is not automatically trustworthy. Scores must preserve uncertainty, evidence lineage, dependence, counter-evidence, and appropriate decision authority. Perspective-specific preferences must not silently rewrite factual evidence.

No production runtime, Java demonstration, or empirical performance claim is included in v1.0.0.

## Next Research Milestones

**v1.1.0 — Controlled Validation:** compare matched-feature baselines, context-aware scoring, directional weak-evidence scoring, and goal-aware decision policies; measure accuracy, calibration, utility, cross-domain transfer, and robustness to manipulated or duplicated reputation signals.

**v1.2.0 — Structural Growth:** evaluate candidate feature discovery, sail discovery, promotion criteria, regression checks, and reversibility of learned structures.

**v2.0.0 — Governed Integration:** investigate interfaces with MDT, CCC, Principle Intelligence, PIRP/PIRU, Collective Structural Learning, and SSI under explicit policy controls.
